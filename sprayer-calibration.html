3. PWM Valve Explained

Understanding Your PWM Valve

PWM stands for Pulse-Width Modulation. Modern sprayers use electronic solenoids at each nozzle that pulse open and closed rapidly — typically 10 times per second.

The "duty cycle" (also called PWM %) is the percentage of each cycle the solenoid stays open:

  • 100% duty cycle = fully open, maximum flow
  • 50% duty cycle = open half the time, half the flow
  • 20% duty cycle = open 20% of the time, ~20% of max flow

More duty cycle = more flow = higher GPA.
Less duty cycle = less flow = lower GPA.

Your PWM rate controller automatically adjusts the duty cycle based on speed and target rate. Most controllers let you adjust a "cal value" (also called calibration factor or flow correction) that trims the output up or down.

  • If your actual GPA is LOWER than target → raise the cal value
  • If your actual GPA is HIGHER than target → lower the cal value

Common PWM systems: Raven Hawkeye, Case IH AIM Command, Capstan PinPoint, John Deere ExactApply, TeeJet DynaJet.

4. How to Calibrate

  1. Fill your tank with clean water to a known volume. Enter it in the "Starting tank volume" field above.
  2. Drive and spray at your normal operating speed and target GPA. Keep speed consistent.
  3. After covering some acres, stop. Check your monitor for acres applied (cumulative) and look at your tank level. Enter both as a new segment.
  4. The page calculates your actual GPA for that segment.
  5. Compare the actual GPA to your target GPA (the one your monitor is set to deliver):
    • If actual is within 5% of target: you're good. No adjustment needed.
    • If actual is too LOW (under-applying): raise your PWM cal value on the controller by about 5% of the current value. Example: if cal is currently 200, try 210.
    • If actual is too HIGH (over-applying): lower your cal value by about 5%. Example: 200 → 190.
  6. Continue spraying. Add another segment every few passes to confirm the adjustment worked.
  7. Repeat adjustments in 5% increments until actual GPA matches target within 5%.

Tips:

  • Calibrate with clean water before adding chemicals
  • Check nozzles for wear or clogs before calibrating; worn nozzles can add 5-15% over their rated flow
  • Recalibrate whenever you change nozzles, target GPA, or travel speed
  • For best accuracy, calibrate over at least 10-20 acres per segment
